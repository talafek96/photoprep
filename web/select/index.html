Groups

⌘-click (or Ctrl-click) adds a frame to a selection, ⇧-click takes a range, ⌘A / Ctrl A takes everything on screen, esc clears. With frames selected, the bar at the bottom groups them and can set a target - up to 2 or exactly 1 - which I read as "out of these, pick that many". It works both ways: I can suggest a group and a count, and you can make your own and tell me a count. Nothing is ever enforced - a target only shows where you are, and you can commit over, under or ignoring it entirely.

Verdict keys apply to the whole selection when there is one, so ⇧-click a run of near-duplicates and press X once.

Adding your own photos

What I show you is a proposal, not the whole world. + add photos opens a folder and puts everything in it on the sheet as ○ Not picked, in its own group — so you can bring in a reference frame, a shot from another day, anything I never saw. You can also drag files straight onto the sheet.

Added frames are marked + yours. A dragged-in file has no path of its own, so it is written to disk as it lands — after that it is as real as anything from the shoot folder and I can build with it. A file already on the sheet is recognised and not duplicated.

Changing groups

Select frames and type a group name: an existing name adds them to it (the button says Add to …), a new one makes a new group. Remove from group takes them out of whichever group you are filtered to. A frame can sit in as many groups as you like, and dropping a photo in while a group is on screen adds it to that group.

Group targets

≤5 is my suggestion — how many I proposed from that group. you ≤1, outlined in amber, is a target you set, which I read as an instruction rather than a guess. ✓≤1 means the count currently matches.

Either way it is only a suggestion: nothing is capped, going over is not an error, and you can commit any number you like.

Scores

A score is relative to its group, never absolute — a 7 at Kasaneiwa and a 7 at Angel Road are not the same claim. Focus a frame and press a digit (0 is ten, ` clears); the panel shows where it now ranks among the scored frames of that group, and the tile shows 8 #1. Sorting by Score in group keeps groups together and ranks inside each. So the useful move is: filter to a group, score its frames against each other, and tell me to take the top few.

Keys

The flag keys follow Lightroom: P pick / keep · X reject / cut · U unpick · plus M for maybe, which Lightroom has no equivalent of.

Space loupe · Z zoom fit ↔ 100% · ←→ move · F filter · ⏎ commit

Inside the loupe: T writes a note on the frame you are looking at, tags and all, without going back to the sheet. S switches whether scrolling zooms or pans.

R reverts a frame to my proposal and clears the 🔒. Pressing a verdict the frame is already in also sends it back to ○ Not picked.
